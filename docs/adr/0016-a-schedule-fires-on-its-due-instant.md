# A Schedule Trigger is identified by its due instant

A Trigger that fires by the clock has no subject Thing, so the query that makes every other birth exactly-once — *no Conversation exists for `(assistantKey, subjectThingId)`* ([ADR-0005](0005-triggers-give-birth-responses-continue.md)) — has nothing to ask about. The obvious substitute is a watermark: remember when the schedule last ran, and fire when the next slot has passed. We rejected it for the reason [ADR-0012](0012-a-conversation-is-an-intent-log.md) exists. A watermark is written *after* the work, so a Runtime that dies between birthing the Conversation and saving the mark births it again on restart — and a schedule that chases an insurer has just chased them twice.

Instead, a Conversation born from the clock records **`scheduledFor`**: the instant the schedule was *due*, never the instant a scan noticed it. Birth is then the same query as everywhere else — *no Conversation exists for `(assistantKey, scheduledFor)`* — and it stays true across a restart, a re-scan, and two scans a second apart. The due instant is computable from the `cron` expression alone, so nothing has to be *remembered* for it to be recomputed identically. `scheduledFor` sits beside `subjectThingId` on the Conversation, and exactly one of the two is set.

Three policies follow, and they are the substance of this decision rather than its mechanics.

**A missed slot is caught up once, not once per slot.** The watcher only ever evaluates the *most recent* due instant that has passed. A daily schedule over a weekend the Runtime spent down therefore asks about Monday and never about Saturday or Sunday, so exactly one Conversation is born. This is not extra code — it is what only evaluating the latest instant already does. It is also the right reading of what a Schedule *is*: a standing instruction about the current state of the world ("review outstanding claims"), not an event log to be replayed. Anything that genuinely must not be missed is a Thing materialising or a `wakeAt`, both of which survive downtime exactly.

**A slot is skipped while the previous one is unfinished.** If a Conversation for an earlier `scheduledFor` on the same Assistant is still `running` or `waiting`, the new slot does not give birth. Two live Conversations for one recurring errand produce two Open Questions the User cannot tell apart, and a User who never answers accumulates one per firing — the runaway that the bounds in [ADR-0010](0010-assistants-declare-their-tools.md)'s neighbourhood exist to prevent. The cost is that one unanswered question stalls the schedule, which is a silence, so it is *recorded*: repeated skipping is warned about the way a pinned watermark is, and the fix is to answer the question rather than anything automatic.

**The cron is evaluated in one configured timezone and stored as a UTC instant.** A household means local time by "every Monday at nine", and daylight saving is precisely where "the same firing" stops being obvious: a 02:30 daily slot happens twice in autumn and not at all in spring. Resolving to a UTC instant *before* it becomes an identity settles both — the doubled hour resolves to one instant and fires once, the missing hour resolves to nothing and does not fire.

## Consequences

- `Conversation_DM` gains an indexed `scheduledFor`, and its form model gains the field ([ADR-0008](0008-every-data-model-has-a-form-model.md)). No new Model: the Conversation is the record that the slot was served, exactly as it is the record that an invoice was handled.
- The watcher grows a seventh scan. It reads `cron` off each enabled Assistant's `schedule` Triggers rather than querying for work, which makes it the only scan whose input is configuration rather than the store.
- `TRIGGER_ELIGIBLE_MODELS` does not apply and is not needed. That allow-list stops the Runtime triggering on its own output, and a schedule births from configuration, so it cannot chain. Every other bound does apply — `paused`, `enabled`, the births-per-hour cap and `maxTurns`.
- A schedule with no matching slot yet, or whose slot is already served, costs one comparison and no query.
- `scheduledFor` is the second thing after a ThingID that identifies a Conversation, so "what gave birth to this?" is answered by which of the two fields is set. Both being empty is a bug, and both being set is a bug.
